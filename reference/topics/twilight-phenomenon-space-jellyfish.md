## What the phenomenon is
- A space jellyfish is sunlight reflected by a high rocket plume at dawn or dusk. The observer is in darkness while the plume is still in direct sunlight (Source 2).
- NASA describes the plume as mostly water vapour and carbon dioxide. It appears as a glowing cloud when the observer is dark and the plume is lit (Source 1).
- A SpaceX Dragon director said the sky is still dark but the Sun lights the plume "as it's in space" (Source 5, as reported by Space.com in 2018).
- The "twilight phenomenon" is a related name. Wikipedia says the exhaust particles condense, freeze and expand in thin upper air, then scatter sunlight (Source 3).
- Wikipedia says the plume can also be lit when the second stage fires or vents. This can be seen over a much wider area (Source 3).

## Time relative to sunrise or sunset
- Wikipedia: the effect typically occurs for launches 30 to 60 minutes before sunrise or after sunset (Source 3). This is the only source I fetched that gives that range as text.
- Calibration case: NASA says a Falcon 9 launch from Cape Canaveral on 2026-03-04 was 52 minutes before sunrise. The second-stage plume was high enough to catch the rising Sun while the photographer was in the dark (Source 1).
- Universe Today: "around an hour" before sunrise or after sunset for the feature as a whole. A launch within a couple of hours of sunrise or sunset is worth attention (Source 6).
- Conflict: Wikipedia says 30 to 60 minutes (Source 3). Universe Today says a launch within a couple of hours is interesting, and describes the glow as occurring around an hour from sunrise or sunset (Source 6). The two sources do not give the same window.
- Conflict on liftoff time of the 2026-03-04 launch: the Spaceflight Now text via AIAA says liftoff at 5:52:20 a.m. EST (Source 8). FOX 35 text on FOX 13 says about 5:43 a.m. (Source 9). I did not check sunrise for that date, so I cannot say which time fits the NASA figure.
- Snippet only: WFTV repeats the 30 to 60 minute window. Fox Weather (Source 10, fetched) says only that twilight timing matters. It says the effect is much less likely in full dark or full day.

## Sun depression angle at the observer
- Not found. No fetched source gives a Sun depression angle for the plume to be visible. All sources use time before sunrise or after sunset.

## Rocket altitude and why the plume expands
- NASA names the second-stage plume (Source 1). It does not give an altitude.
- ClickOrlando says hot exhaust cools fast in cold, thin air and forms ice crystals and vapour clouds (Source 7). It calls them noctilucent clouds. This is a news explanation, not a measurement.
- Fox Weather says water vapour freezes as the rocket climbs and forms ice crystals (Source 10).
- Wikipedia says the exhaust expands in less dense upper air (Source 3).
- Not found: a measured altitude at which the plume first becomes visible, or a measured expansion rate. No peer-reviewed source on launch-plume twilight visibility was found.

## How long it stays visible
- Wikipedia: usually seen within two to three minutes after launch. It can stay up to half an hour, depending on weather (Source 3).
- ClickOrlando: bright plumes often linger about 30 to 60 minutes (Source 7).
- Universe Today: noctilucent-type clouds after the show may persist up to an hour (Source 6).
- Conflict: up to half an hour (Source 3) versus 30 to 60 minutes (Source 7).
- The glow moves in the sky. NASA says it only looks as if it falls, because the rocket follows the Earth's curve (Source 1).

## How far away it has been seen
- Wikipedia: on the East Coast, sightings were reported after twilight Space Shuttle launches from KSC and after other launches from Cape Canaveral (Source 3). It gives no distance.
- Universe Today: the author has seen launch jellyfish as far inland as Asheville, North Carolina. He also shows photos from Cary, North Carolina and Virginia Beach (Source 6).
- FOX 35 meteorologist: the 2026-03-04 effect was likely seen in Georgia and along the northeast coast (Source 9). This is a "likely", not a report.
- Snippet only: a Space.com item (via AIAA) says the Starlink 10-49 jellyfish on 2026-08-25 was seen as far as New York City.
- Snippet only: PetaPixel claims about 1,000 miles.
- Not found in these sources: a confirmed sighting from Charleston. See reference/topics/charleston-sighting-reports.md, written by another agent, for that question.

## What this means for Space Jellyfish
This section is interpretation, not sourced fact.
- Use "launch within about 60 minutes of sunrise or sunset" only as a rough pre-filter, because the sources give a time window and not a physical rule. The physical rule belongs in the geometry note (reference/topics/twilight-definitions-and-earth-shadow.md).
- Do not hard-code a Sun depression limit from these sources. None exists in them. Derive it from the shadow height at the plume position and test it against known sightings.
- Treat the 2026-03-04 case (52 minutes before sunrise, northeast launch) as one positive calibration point for the backtest.
- Keep the visible duration as a soft output. Use 30 minutes as a low value and 60 minutes as a high value, and show both. The sources conflict.
- Confidence: low for numeric thresholds. The sources are news and encyclopedia text with no measurements.
